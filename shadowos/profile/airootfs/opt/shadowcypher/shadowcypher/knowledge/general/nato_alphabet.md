## Common Uses

- Spelling out passwords, codes, callsigns, and identifiers over radio/phone
- Avoiding confusion between similar-sounding letters (B/D/E/G/P/T/V)
- Aviation, military, emergency services, maritime communication
- Cybersecurity incident response communications (reading out IP addresses, hashes)

## Example Usage

Password `P@ssw0rd` spoken as:
"Papa at-sign Sierra Sierra Whiskey Zero Romeo Delta"

IP Address 192.168.1.100:
"Wun Niner Two dot Wun Six Eight dot Wun dot Wun Zero Zero"

## History

Adopted by NATO in 1956; used internationally by ICAO (aviation), IMO (maritime), and emergency services worldwide.
Replaced earlier alphabets (Able Baker, 1943 Joint Army/Navy) to improve intelligibility across languages and radio static.
